id: INV-038
statement: quiet_stdout_logs's module-level lock plus depth counter ensures
  only the outermost caller (across every thread) ever saves or restores the
  stdout handler's log level -- nested or concurrent callers just bump depth
criticality: high
evidence:
  - tests/unit/test_logging_quiet.py::TestQuietStdoutLogsReentrance::test_nested_calls_restore_after_outermost_exits
  - tests/unit/test_logging_quiet.py::TestQuietStdoutLogsReentrance::test_interleaved_enter_exit_across_threads_never_sticks
  - tests/unit/test_logging_quiet.py::TestQuietStdoutLogsReentrance::test_threaded_stress_always_restores
  - tests/unit/test_logging_quiet.py::TestQuietStdoutLogsReentrance::test_single_call_still_quiets_and_restores

`frob.logging.quiet.quiet_stdout_logs` mutates the shared, process-global
root logger's stdout handler level. T-0125 fixed a race where two
concurrent callers (e.g. `frob.arch.analyze_project` and
`frob.dup.find_duplicates`, both invoked from `frob check`'s
`ThreadPoolExecutor`) could leave the handler permanently stuck at WARNING:
whichever caller entered second would "save" the already-lowered level and
then restore it, leaving INFO/DEBUG logs silently swallowed downstream with
no exception and no trace.

The fix is a module-level `threading.Lock` plus a reentrancy depth counter:
only the outermost caller across all threads saves the pre-existing level
on entry and restores it on exit; every nested or concurrently-racing
caller only increments/decrements the depth. If this ever regresses (e.g.
a refactor drops the lock or double-counts depth), the handler can again
get stuck at the wrong level with no visible symptom until a later `frob
check` run silently loses its INFO/DEBUG log lines.

`TestQuietStdoutLogsReentrance` proves this directly: nested calls restore
only once, at the true outermost exit; interleaved enter/exit sequences
across two real threads never leave the handler "stuck"; and a
higher-volume threaded stress test (many workers entering/exiting
concurrently) always converges back to the original level once every
caller has exited.
