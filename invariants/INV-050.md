This is strictly STRONGER than INV-003 (rebuildability): INV-003 only
requires that DELETING a cache be safe (the source can always reproduce it).
This invariant additionally requires that KEEPING a stale-but-present cache
around never silently changes what a caller observes -- the failure mode
INV-003 does not catch, and the exact shape of the real T-1454 incident (`frob
ack` rewrote `frob.lock`, no tracked source digest changed, a cached DRIFT001
result went stale and kept being served).

## The shared harness

`tests/_cache_transparency.py::run_cold_warm_sweep` generalizes
`test_gate_cache.py::TestColdDiffOracle`'s randomized multi-round
mutate-and-compare walk into one reusable driver: given a `mutate(rng,
round_index)` callback (the edit vocabulary -- touch/add/remove/rename/
revert/no-op) and a `cold_fingerprint()`/`warm_fingerprint()` pair, it
asserts the two fingerprints agree after EVERY round, not just at the end,
so a disagreement is attributed to the exact round (and edit kind) that
caused it. `tests/test_cache_transparency.py` parameterizes it over the
graph cache (`.frob/cache.db`), the pytest-collection cache
(`.frob/pytest-collect.json`), the coverage lock
(`frob-coverage.lock.json`), the hot-graph sketch store
(`.frob/hotgraph_sketches.db`), and the `--budget` timing estimate
(`.frob/check-budget-timing.json`, T-1529); `test_gate_cache.py`'s own
`TestColdDiffOracle` predates the shared driver and is left as-is (its
existing `cold`/`warm` loop is functionally identical, just written before
this module existed to hold it).

As of T-1529, every cache in the inventory above is exercised by the
harness -- the three caches T-1521/T-1519 originally left as a disclosed
cut (the coverage lock, the hot-graph sketch store, and the `--budget`
timing estimate) now each have their own `TestXCacheTransparency` sweep,
closing out T-1519's deliverable (3) in full: "every cache either covered
by the harness or carrying a frob:waive naming a ticket" now reads simply
as "every cache covered by the harness." `INV050` is not itself a
registered gate rule id (this is a design invariant proven by dedicated
tests, not a mechanically single-anchor-checked one the way INV001/
INV002/INV005 are).
