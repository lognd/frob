---
id: INV-005
statement: Every raw recursive filesystem traversal in src/frob/ is caught
  by WALK001 unless it goes through the shared prune-aware helpers
criticality: medium
evidence:
  - tests/test_walk_lint_gate.py::TestRglob::test_raw_rglob_fires
  - tests/test_walk_lint_gate.py::TestHelper::test_helper_call_is_silent
  - tests/test_walk_lint_gate.py::TestSelfMatchExclusion::test_own_files_not_scanned
---

`frob.excludes.walk_pruned`/`iter_files` are the one sanctioned way to walk
a project tree in `src/frob/`: they prune `.git`/`.venv`/`node_modules`/
build output and any `frob.toml`-declared exclude globs before descending.
A new call site that reaches for `Path.rglob`, `os.walk`, or a recursive
`glob.glob("**"...)` directly bypasses that pruning silently -- it would
still work most of the time, then blow up (or slow to a crawl) the first
time it wanders into `.venv` or `node_modules` in a real project.

`walk_lint_gate` (WALK001) statically scans every git-tracked
`src/frob/**/*.py` file's AST for exactly that bypass pattern and fails the
gate on any hit that isn't the shared helper itself or a per-line
`frob:waive WALK001 reason="..."` escape hatch.

`test_raw_rglob_fires` proves a raw `Path(...).rglob(...)` call is caught;
`test_helper_call_is_silent` proves a call that goes through the shared
helper is not flagged (no false positive on the sanctioned path);
`test_own_files_not_scanned` proves the gate module's own necessarily-raw
implementation does not trip its own rule. Together they cover both
directions of the claim: the bypass is caught, and the sanctioned path is
not.
