---
id: INV-025
statement: An obfuscation signal (high-entropy string, bidi override, hex
  identifier ratio) found anywhere under a dependency's source directory
  always surfaces as a VET004 error -- obfuscation has no [vet.allow]
  declaration path, unlike native/other capabilities
criticality: high
evidence:
  - tests/test_vet.py::TestObfuscationEnsemble::test_high_entropy_string_flagged
  - tests/test_vet.py::TestObfuscationEnsemble::test_bidi_override_is_fatal
  - tests/test_vet.py::TestObfuscationEnsemble::test_scan_directory_obfuscation_finds_signal_in_one_file
---

`_scan_directory_obfuscation` unions every obfuscation signal found across
a dependency's source tree; `_capability_and_fingerprint_signals` appends
a `_vet004_violation` (ERROR severity) unconditionally whenever that union
is non-empty (or a decode-to-exec co-occurrence fired) -- there is no
`[vet.allow]` lookup in that path the way `VET001`'s uncovered-dependency
check has. Unlike `native`/other declared capabilities, an obfuscation
finding cannot be silenced by declaring it in `frob.toml`; the only
remedies are removing the dependency or the offending code.

`test_high_entropy_string_flagged` and `test_bidi_override_is_fatal` prove
the individual signal detectors fire on real obfuscated content;
`test_scan_directory_obfuscation_finds_signal_in_one_file` proves the
directory-level union surfaces a signal found in any single file, which is
what `_capability_and_fingerprint_signals` turns into an unconditional
VET004.
